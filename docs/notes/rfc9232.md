# RFC 9232 — Network Telemetry Framework

IETF OPSAWG, Informational, May 2022. Source: `scratchpad/rfc/rfc9232.txt` (read in full,
including Appendix A).

## 1. Purpose

RFC 9232 gives network telemetry an architecture so that the many techniques (SNMP,
syslog, IPFIX, YANG-Push, gNMI, BMP, IOAM, Alternate Marking, postcards) can be placed
and combined. It defines telemetry as both the data and the processes to generate,
export, collect and consume it, distinguishes it from classical OAM (subscription and
streaming, machine consumers, model-based, high volume), lists the use cases that drive
it (security, policy and intent compliance, SLA compliance, root cause analysis,
optimization, event tracking), explains why SNMP/CLI/syslog polling is insufficient, and
then partitions telemetry into four modules by data source (management plane, control
plane, forwarding plane, external data), each with the same five components (query/
analysis/storage, configuration and subscription, encoding and export, generation and
processing, data object and source). It abstracts acquisition into subscription (push)
vs query (poll) and data into simple, derived, event-triggered and streaming; sketches an
evolution from static to closed-loop telemetry; and lists security concerns. It defines
no protocol. For Arcana it is the frame for the agent's events, history, probes, record
and captures and for what to add (flow export, standard subscriptions, active
measurement).

## 2. Concepts and definitions, completely

### 2.1 Applicability and glossary (Sections 1.1, 1.2)

Applicability: large-scale collection may be indistinguishable from pervasive
monitoring (RFC 7258); the framework **must not** be applied to generating, exporting,
collecting, analyzing or retaining individual user data or data that identifies end
users or characterizes their behaviour without consent; not applicable to networks whose
endpoints represent individual users (general-purpose access networks). No user packet
payload is to be collected (2.1).

Glossary: AI; AM (Alternate Marking, RFC 8321); BMP (RFC 7854); DPI; gNMI (gRPC Network
Management Interface, OpenConfig); GPB; gRPC; IPFIX (RFC 7011); IOAM (RFC 9197, in-band
on-path telemetry); JSON; MIB; NETCONF; NetFlow (RFC 3954); **Network Telemetry** (the
process and instrumentation for acquiring and utilizing network data remotely for
monitoring and operation; an umbrella term from generation to consumption; enables
intent-driven autonomous networks); NMS; OAM (fault indication, localization,
performance information, diagnosis; conventional monitoring); PBT (Postcard-Based
Telemetry: IOAM Direct Export); RESTCONF (RFC 8040); SMIv2; SNMP; XML; YANG; YANG ECA
(event-condition-action YANG model, draft); YANG-Push (RFC 8639 subscriptions to YANG
notifications, RFC 8641 datastore update subscriptions).

### 2.2 Background (Section 2)

- Telemetry data coverage (2.1): anything extractable from data, control and management
  planes used for visibility or action: statistics, event records and logs, snapshots of
  state, configuration data, outputs of active and passive measurements (RFC 7799),
  in-network processed data. Less but higher-quality data may be preferable.
- Use cases (2.2): **Security** (IDS/IPS acting on anomalies with minimal human
  intervention); **Policy and Intent Compliance** (continuous verification, immediate
  violation reporting, feeding back into how policy/intent is applied); **SLA
  Compliance** (metrics and remedies, real-time measurement data); **Root Cause
  Analysis** (chained events, sporadic failures, overwhelming event volume); **Network
  Optimization** (load balancing, TE, planning; microburst detection; long-term
  capacity); **Event Tracking and Prediction** (where/why packets are dropped, early
  warning).
- Challenges (2.3): poll-based low-frequency collection unsuited; subscription-based
  streaming from the source preferred; comprehensive data from packet engines to
  control boards needed; correlation across sources; CLI and syslog lack formal data
  models; existing push (SNMP traps, syslog, sFlow) is limited to predefined warnings or
  sampled packets; passive measurement is costly or inaccurate, active measurement
  intrusive and indirect. Addressed by IPFIX/NetFlow, PSAMP, IOAM, YANG-Push and more.
- Characteristics of network telemetry (2.4): **push and streaming**; **volume and
  velocity** (machines consume); **normalization and unification**; **model-based**;
  **data fusion** (common names/IDs across sources); **dynamic and interactive** (closed
  loop, adapts to controller queries). Ideal features: in-network customization
  (programmable data plane), in-network aggregation and correlation, in-network
  processing (local reaction), direct data plane export, in-band data collection
  (IFIT). Must avoid the observer effect and congestion (RFC 8084 circuit breakers,
  RFC 8085 UDP guidelines). No architectural assumption: producers and consumers may be
  peer-to-peer; a node can consume another node's telemetry.
- Necessity of a framework (2.5): holistic visibility; multiple viewpoints (device vs
  traffic) that applications switch between; elasticity (low rate routinely, boosted on
  issues); efficient aggregation; consolidate protocols into a minimum comprehensive set.

### 2.3 The framework (Section 3)

Two levels: four modules by data source (3.1), each decomposed into five components
(3.2), with uniform acquisition mechanisms and data types (3.3).

Figure 1 (modules): Network Operation Applications on top; Management Plane Telemetry,
Control Plane Telemetry and Forwarding Plane Telemetry in one block (control plane
telemetry may need the FIB from the forwarding plane; management plane telemetry
acquires from the data plane, e.g. interface status); External Data and Event Telemetry
beside them; each module has its own interface to the applications. A controller may
itself be a telemetry source, out of scope.

Table 1, comparison of modules:

| | Management plane | Control plane | Forwarding plane | External data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Object | configuration and operation state | control protocol and signaling, RIB | flow and packet QoS, traffic statistics, buffer and queue statistics, FIB, ACL | terminal, social, environmental |
| Export location | main control CPU | main control CPU, linecard CPU or forwarding chip | forwarding chip or linecard CPU; main CPU unlikely | various |
| Data model | YANG, MIB, syslog | YANG, custom | YANG, custom | YANG, custom |
| Encoding | GPB, JSON, XML | GPB, JSON, XML, plain text | plain text | GPB, JSON, XML, plain text |
| Application protocol | gRPC, NETCONF, RESTCONF | gRPC, NETCONF, IPFIX, traffic mirroring | IPFIX, traffic mirroring, gRPC, NetFlow | gRPC |
| Transport | HTTP(S), TCP | HTTP(S), TCP, UDP | UDP | HTTP(S), TCP, UDP |

"One cannot expect to use a universal protocol to cover all the network telemetry
requirements."

Per-module requirements:
- **Management plane (3.1.1)**: convenient data subscription (choose what, on-change or
  periodic); structured data (YANG); high-speed transport (compact encoding,
  compression, subscription instead of query); congestion avoidance (RFC 8084/8085).
- **Control plane (3.1.2)**: health of control protocols at all layers; correlate
  end-to-end KPIs to a layer, protocol and device; conventional ping/traceroute/Y.1731
  only measure KPIs, not protocol status; BMP is the example (peer up/down, Adj-RIB-In/
  Out, Loc-RIB, stats); IGP monitoring immature.
- **Forwarding plane (3.1.3)**: do not impede forwarding; volume must not exhaust
  bandwidth; minimum delay; structured, labeled, flexible data; incremental deployment;
  congestion avoidance. Technique taxonomy: *active, passive, hybrid* (passive: tcpdump,
  IPFIX, sFlow, mirroring, low coverage or high cost; active: ping, OWAMP RFC 4656,
  TWAMP RFC 5357, STAMP RFC 8762, Cisco SLA RFC 6812, intrusive and indirect; hybrid:
  IOAM, AM RFC 8321, multipoint AM RFC 8889); *in-band vs out-of-band* (IOAM in packets
  vs postcards exported directly; AM hybrid); *end-to-end vs in-network*; *data subject*
  (flow-based IOAM, path-based traceroute, node-based IPFIX; objects packet, flow
  record, measurement, state, signal).
- **External data (3.1.4)**: hardware sensors and software big-data sources; a common
  but extensible schema mappable to YANG; timeliness and priority; congestion
  countermeasures over the Internet.

Five components (3.2, Figure 2): **Data Query, Analysis and Storage** (application side:
issues requirements — one-shot queries or subscriptions, modeled or programmed data;
receives, stores, processes; may be in devices or controllers, centralized or
distributed); **Data Configuration and Subscription** (protocol and channel selection;
configuring data not directly available; described by models, templates or programs);
**Data Encoding and Export** (delivery with access control; encoding and transport vary
by location); **Data Generation and Processing** (capture, filter, process, format;
fast or slow path); **Data Object and Source** (monitoring objects and raw sources,
each a probe; static or dynamically installed).

Acquisition and types (3.3): **subscription** (contract until expiry; predefined or
tailored) vs **query** (immediate one-off; interactive). Four data types: **Simple**
(steadily available from a datastore or static probe), **Derived** (synthesized in the
network from one or more devices; processing statically or dynamically loaded),
**Event-triggered** (conditional on an event such as interface up/down; FSM or ECA
modeling; pushed or polled), **Streaming** (continuously generated: time series or
database dumps, e.g. counters every second; always pushed). Composite relations: derived
of simple; event-triggered simple or derived; streaming based on recurring events.
Subscription usually for event-triggered and streaming, query for simple and derived.

Table 2, existing work mapped: data configuration/subscription: gNMI, NETCONF, RESTCONF,
SNMP, YANG-Push (all planes); generation/processing: MIB, YANG (MP), YANG (CP), IOAM,
PSAMP, PBT, AM (FP); encoding/export: gRPC, HTTP, TCP (MP), BMP, TCP (CP), IPFIX, UDP
(FP). Multi-domain telemetry out of scope.

### 2.4 Evolution (Section 4), security (Section 5), Appendix A

Stages: 0 static (source and type fixed at design time), 1 dynamic (custom data
programmed at runtime without interruption), 2 interactive (continuous real-time
tuning, human in the middle), 3 closed-loop (the engine issues requests, analyzes,
updates operations; humans only read reports). Existing technology covers 0 and 1;
individual 2 and 3 applications exist.

Security: telemetry can exhaust resources at every plane and at the consumer;
falsified data misleads decisions; wrong configuration is harmful; telemetry is highly
sensitive (software and patch details aid attackers). Considerations: trust and policy
models; role management and access control for enabling/disabling telemetry; transport
security; data stores, encryption, access methods, retention; tracking telemetry events
and abnormalities; authentication and integrity of data; segregating telemetry traffic
from data traffic (management network). Classes of policies: RBAC and ECA; conflicts
between telemetry mechanisms must be detected to avoid self-inflicted DoS.

Appendix A survey: **YANG-Push** (RFC 8639/8641, continuous customized stream from a
YANG datastore, remote mirroring of config and operational state; distributed
notifications and UDP publication channel drafts); **gNMI** (gRPC over HTTP/2: one
service for config and telemetry, full-duplex streaming, binary encoding, platform
consistency, load balancing); **BMP** (TCP session, Peer Up/Down, Route Monitoring and
Mirroring for Adj-RIB-In (7854), Adj-RIB-Out (8671), Loc-RIB (9069), Stats Report);
**Alternate Marking** (batches by flipping a bit; two counters per flow per period; loss
and delay; multipoint clustering for coarse-to-fine monitoring); **Dynamic Network
Probe** (programmable data plane probes, API undefined); **IPFIX** (Metering Processes
at Observation Points, Exporter, Observation Domain, Collector); **IOAM** (instruction
header in user packets, nodes append data; performance, security, overhead, encapsulation
and cross-domain challenges); **Postcard-Based Telemetry** (IOAM DEX / marking; per-node
export packets; locates drops); **OAM for specific data planes** (MPLS, L2VPN, NVO3,
VXLAN, BIER, SFC, SR, DetNet); **External events** (smart objects/sensors over CoAP;
online news such as CVE feeds in relaxed structured formats; global event analyzers
needing new information models) and **connectors** (via the management plane with YANG
and NETCONF/YANG-Push/gRPC).

### 3.2 Gaps against the framework

1. Data is **not model-based**: JSON blobs inside CBOR, shapes documented in prose in
   `arcana.yaml` `doc:` strings. Normalization across hosts works only because there is
   one implementation.
2. **Subscriptions are one-size**: all events or none; no filter by kind/object, no
   periodic subscription, no dampening or rate negotiation per subscriber; history is
   polled.
3. **No flow-level forwarding-plane telemetry**: which flows use which tunnel, top
   talkers, per-application volumes for SASE and for dynamic mesh decisions (rfc7018.md)
   are unknown beyond rule counters.
4. **Active measurement is ICMP**: round trip halved; no one-way delay, no timestamps
   at the reflector, ICMP may be deprioritized by underlays. The RFC lists STAMP
   (RFC 8762) as the modern active method.
5. **Control-plane telemetry of BGP is polled through birdc** text; no BMP stream.
6. **No standard export** (IPFIX, YANG-Push, gNMI, syslog over TLS) for a third-party
   collector; the console is the only consumer.
7. **Telemetry and configuration share one transport and priority**; the security
   section asks for segregation and role-based control of telemetry.
8. **Retention and storage** are ad hoc (512 events, 24 h faults, a day of record,
   thinned history); no long-term store for root cause analysis.

### 3.3 Proposal for Arcana on FreeBSD

1. **Model the telemetry** with the YANG redesign: `ietf-interfaces` statistics,
   `ietf-routing` RIB, an `arcana-telemetry` module for history samples (a time series
   list with thinning metadata), `arcana-probe` (MEF 105 metrics), `arcana-faults`
   (Mplify 119 Table 5 attributes), events as YANG notifications with a defined
   `kind`/object schema, record and IDS alerts as notifications.
2. **YANG-Push semantics over cblink**: a `subscribe` operation with RFC 8639/8641
   parameters (datastore, xpath/subtree filter, periodic with period and anchor time or
   on-change with dampening period, replay start from event number or time, stop time,
   encoding) opening a cblink *push stream* (`CBL_OPF_PUSH`, server-to-client, credit
   flow control = per-subscription back-pressure). Keep the `events` multicast group as
   the unfiltered legacy; map the ring to RFC 8639 replay (`replay-start-time`, the
   "first" number telling when replay is incomplete). Periodic subscriptions replace
   polling of history and stats. Support `kill-subscription`, `modify-subscription`,
   and `subscription-terminated` notifications when the agent sheds load (circuit
   breaker per RFC 8084).
3. **Forwarding-plane flow export** with netgraph: `ng_ipfw(4)` lets ipfw rules
   `ngtee`/`netgraph` packets into a netgraph node; attach `ng_netflow(4)` per service
   (observation domain = service, per-zone rule numbers as hooks) and export NetFlow v5
   (`export` hook) or v9 (`export9` hook) over `ng_ksocket` UDP to a collector. Verify
   on the lab whether the installed ng_netflow emits IPFIX (v10); its manual documents
   v5 and v9, so plan for a v9 collector or a userland converter on the console host
   (pmacct/nfacctd can receive v9 and re-export IPFIX). pflow(4) exports IPFIX directly
   but only for pf state, which Arcana does not use. Expose flow summaries (top flows
   per tunnel, per application flow) in the `sdwan` read and feed them to the steering
   manager and to dynamic mesh decisions. Sampling and no payload: RFC 9232 privacy
   rule.
4. **STAMP (RFC 8762) probes** beside ICMP: implement Session-Sender and
   Session-Reflector in the agent (small UDP protocol, NTP/PTP timestamps, one-way delay
   both directions, authenticated mode with a key from the console); run them over the
   tunnels and between UNIs for end-to-end flow SLOs (rfc9315.md). Keep BFD for up/down.
5. **BMP from BIRD**: BIRD supports a `bmp` protocol; point every service instance at a
   BMP station on the console host (or an agent-side relay) for peer up/down and RIB
   mirroring, replacing polled `show protocols` for control-plane telemetry; keep birdc
   for commands. ipfw `log logdst rtsock` and netlink stay the kernel-side event sources.
6. **Standard NBI telemetry at the console**: RESTCONF with YANG-Push over HTTPS
   (RFC 8650) and the existing WebSocket; optional IPFIX/NetFlow pass-through and syslog
   over TLS (RFC 5425) relaying from the console for external collectors; bsnmpd for
   legacy NMS. gNMI/gRPC is not natural in C/Kore and is not required by the framework.
7. **Segregation and roles**: telemetry subscriptions on their own cblink stream (SCTP
   stream) or connection; token roles that allow telemetry subscription without
   configuration rights; per-subscription rate limits; an audit event when telemetry is
   enabled/disabled or its rate changed; once an ACP exists (rfc8994.md), telemetry
   rides the ACP, which is the "independent management network" the RFC mentions.
8. **Storage**: the console keeps a compact time-series store (append-only files per
   host per metric, downsampled like the agent's thinning) with a retention setting, so
   root cause analysis across a reboot is possible; the agent's rings stay the replay
   buffers.
9. **External events**: ingest `pkg audit` / VuXML results as external security events
   per host (the agent already runs pkg), accept signed webhooks at the console as
   external event sources for workflows, and model them as YANG notifications with a
   common envelope (time, source, severity, object).
10. **Privacy guard rails**: captures and record keep addresses and headers only;
    no payload retention beyond the admin-triggered pcap; document that SASE identities
    (remote users) are infrastructure identities under the operator's consent.

## 4. Cannot be mapped / not applicable

- **IOAM, Postcard-Based Telemetry, Alternate Marking**: require data-plane support
  for extension headers or marking bits in forwarding chips; FreeBSD's IP stack has no
  IOAM and ipfw cannot colour and count by AM period without kernel work. Not planned.
- **Dynamic Network Probes / programmable data plane**: no P4-like data plane; netmap
  or divert(4) to userland could emulate but at a forwarding cost the RFC warns about.
- **gNMI/gRPC/GPB**: a different ecosystem; cblink/CBOR fills the same role internally.
- **sFlow**: no FreeBSD sampler in base; ng_netflow with sampling is the substitute.
- **In-band data collection (IFIT)**: not applicable.
- **Forwarding-chip export location**: all export is from the host CPU; Table 1's
  "forwarding chip" column collapses onto the main CPU for Arcana.
- **End-user access networks**: SASE remote users are the one place where the
  applicability statement bites; flow export must exclude or aggregate their traffic.

## 5. Checklist Arcana could be measured against

1. Telemetry data is described by a formal model (YANG) with stable names shared by
   agent, console and NBI.
2. Consumers can subscribe (periodic and on-change) with filters, dampening and replay,
   and can modify or terminate subscriptions.
3. Event-triggered data is pushed within one second of the event and carries a sequence
   number allowing loss detection and replay.
4. Streaming data (counters, samples) is pushed, not polled, at a subscriber-chosen
   period.
5. Bulk data uses flow control so that a slow consumer cannot exhaust the producer or
   the network (exists: cblink credits).
6. Forwarding-plane telemetry includes flow records (NetFlow/IPFIX) with sampling and
   without payload, per service and zone.
7. Active measurement uses a standard protocol with one-way metrics (STAMP) in
   addition to ICMP, and BFD for liveness.
8. Control-plane telemetry streams protocol state (BMP for BGP; netlink events for RIB
   and interfaces; BFD state) rather than polling text output.
9. Management-plane telemetry covers configuration state, logs (syslog, kmsg), events,
   resource usage and job traces.
10. Telemetry can be exported to a third-party collector with standard protocols
    (IPFIX/NetFlow, YANG-Push over RESTCONF, syslog) and standard models.
11. Telemetry is rate-limited and protected by a circuit breaker; subscriptions shed load
    predictably and tell the subscriber.
12. Telemetry access is authorized by role, separate from configuration rights, and
    changes to telemetry configuration are audited.
13. Telemetry traffic can be segregated from data traffic (dedicated stream/connection,
    ACP).
14. Retention periods are explicit and configurable per data class; long-term storage
    supports root cause analysis.
15. No end-user payload is collected; address-level records are documented and bounded.
16. Timestamps come from a synchronized clock (NTP step exists) and are carried in every
    record.
17. The system can raise telemetry rates on faults and lower them in steady state
    (elasticity).
18. Closed loops (failover, TCA-driven actions) are documented with their inputs and
    reaction times.

## 6. Open decisions and trade-offs

- **NetFlow v9 vs IPFIX**: depends on what ng_netflow actually exports on the target
  FreeBSD; a userland converter adds a component; pf/pflow would give IPFIX natively but
  changing firewalls is out of the question.
- **Where flow records go**: to the console host (one collector, more WAN traffic) or
  stay on the edge and only summaries travel (in-network aggregation, less visibility).
  Likely both: summaries always, raw records on demand or sampled.
- **STAMP vs keeping ICMP only**: STAMP needs the reflector on the far edge (we control
  both ends, fine) and clock sync for one-way delay; the gain is accuracy and
  directionality, the cost is a new daemon thread and key management.
- **BMP**: depends on the installed BIRD version; polling stays as fallback.
- **YANG-Push over cblink vs a second protocol**: cblink streams already have the right
  properties; the risk is inventing a dialect. Mitigation: copy RFC 8639/8641
  parameter names and semantics exactly.
- **Storage on the console**: files vs a small database; retention vs disk on a jail
  host.
- **Telemetry over the ACP** (once it exists) vs over the data plane: the ACP gives
  reachability when the data plane is broken (the moment telemetry matters most) but
  may have lower bandwidth; send events and faults over the ACP, bulk over the data
  plane.
