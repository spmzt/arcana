# RFC 8578 — Deterministic Networking Use Cases

Informational, May 2019. Grossman (ed.), 19 contributors.
Local copy: `scratchpad/rfc/rfc8578.txt`.

## 1. Purpose

RFC 8578 documents the industries that need "deterministic flows" (guaranteed bandwidth,
bounded latency, bounded jitter, very low loss, high availability, often precise time) over
multi-hop packet networks, as context for DetNet design decisions and as a yardstick for
proposed designs. For each of nine use cases — professional audio/video, electrical
utilities, building automation, wireless industrial (6TiSCH), cellular radio (fronthaul,
midhaul, backhaul), industrial machine-to-machine, mining, private blockchain, and network
slicing — it states what the use case is, how it is served today, what the future should
look like, and what it asks of the IETF, with concrete numbers (latency, jitter, loss,
availability, recovery). Section 11 distils the common themes (unified standards-based
network, central administration, information models, L2/L3 integration, IPv4, guaranteed
delivery, OT/IT mix, unused reservations reusable, scalable flows, bounded/low latency,
bounded jitter, symmetric delay, high availability, security, isolation). Appendix A lists
what was declared out of scope (open Internet applications, DRM, link aggregation,
deterministic stream-setup time) and the scope principles: centrally controlled
enterprise networks only, time sync by other means.

## 2. Use cases, terminology and the numbers they state (complete)

### 2.1 Professional audio and video (§2)
Content creation, broadcast, cinema, live sound, public address/emergency at large venues. Needs: uninterrupted stream playback (no retries possible; guaranteed bandwidth removes congestion loss; redundant paths; FEC), synchronised playback (A/V sync within one NTSC frame ≈ 33 ms, consistent within ~10 %; sinks buffer to the slowest path; common time reference; often a central controller determining path delays), sound reinforcement (total latency 10–15 ms including everything, so network ≪ 15 ms; phase-critical beam-forming ~10 µs = one 96 kHz sample), secure transmission (amplifier control traffic). Today: proprietary engineered L3 networks, over-provisioned; TSN only inside L2 islands (ESPN DC2: seven TSN studios interconnected by a custom SDN router). Future: L3 interconnecting L2 islands, high-reliability redundant stream paths, integration into IT networks with commodity gear, unused reservations usable by best effort, traffic segregation for low-cost sinks (forwarding rules, VLANs/subnets, multicast with one IPv4 group per MAC), latency optimisation by a central controller (move reservations to longer paths when sinks can buffer), reduced buffer memory at sinks with one buffering gateway. Requests: L3 routing over AVB/high-QoS networks, bounded lowest-possible latency, IntServ/DiffServ integration with AVB, one network for A/V and IT, standards-based multi-vendor, IT-friendly, enterprise-wide (city-sized).

### 2.2 Electrical utilities (§3)
- **Teleprotection (§3.1.1.1)**: criteria of IEC 60834 — transmission time, dependability (PMC), security (PUC). Fault clearance ≤ 100 ms total (five cycles), protection operates in 70–80 % of it, leaving ~10 ms for telecom; IEC 61850-5:2013 asks 4–8 ms for the most critical. Symmetric channel delay for differential protection (legacy tolerance 750 µs asymmetry); tools: TDM jitter buffers, highest priority on IP, PTP/syncE. Tables 1–6: one-way max delay 4–10 ms (teleprotection) / 5 ms (inter-trip, current differential, distance, inter-/intra-substation); asymmetric delay required for teleprotection and current differential; max jitter < 250 µs (750 µs legacy) where jitter matters; topology P2P and P2MP; bandwidth 64 kbit/s; availability 99.9999 %; precise timing yes; recovery on node failure < 50 ms hitless; performance management mandatory; redundancy yes; loss 0.1 %–1 %.
- **WAMS/synchrophasors (§3.1.1.3, Table 7)**: 50 ms one-way, 100 kbit/s, MP2MP, 99.9999 %, precise timing, < 50 ms hitless recovery, loss 1 %, at least one packet per application cycle must arrive.
- **IEC TR 61850-90-12 WAN classes (Table 8)**: WA (EHV): 5 ms latency, 10 µs jitter, 100 µs asymmetry, 1 µs time accuracy, BER 10^-7–10^-6, zero recovery delay; WB (HV): 10 ms, 100 µs, 1 ms, 10 µs, 50 ms recovery; WC (MV): 100 ms, 1 ms, 10 ms, 100 µs, 5 s; WD (general): > 100 ms, 10 ms, 100 ms, 10–100 ms, 50 s.
- **Generation (§3.1.2)**: FCAG/AGC 500 ms, 20 kbit/s, P2P, 99.999 %, loss 1 % (Table 9). Wind turbine subsystems (Table 10 data rates up to ~74 kB/s per subsystem; Table 11: analog/status/control/polling 16 ms, protection 4 ms with 100 % reliability and < 10^-9 loss, reporting 1 s, video 1 s, Internet 60 min). Intra-domain: fibre, industrial buses, Modbus/PROFIBUS/PROFINET/EtherCAT; inter-domain over the Internet today best effort (IEC 60870-5-104, OPC XML-DA, Modbus, SNMP), future bounded latency/jitter over operator WAN for SCADA failover between parks.
- **Distribution FLISR (§3.1.3, Table 12)**: 80 ms, 40 ms jitter, 64 kbit/s, MP2MP, 99.9999 %, loss 0.1 %.
- Today: siloed proprietary TDM networks, no OT/IT mixing, costly calibration; security weaknesses of request/response protocols (DNP3, Modbus: no auth/encryption, dangerous commands, DoS by broadcast, scans). Future: one IP (IPv6 per IEC TR 62357-200) multi-service network, cloud SCADA, MPLS WANs; telecom trends list (IP everywhere, monitoring, virtual DC, fibre+microwave, IEEE 1588, multicast, QoS mapping, SLAs, 3G/4G, station/process bus Ethernet, protection/PMUs on IP); two physically diverse networks (optical + microwave) with a circuit on each, path diversity key; PTP (IEEE 1588, IEC/IEEE 61850-9-3 power profile, PRP/HSR attachment) replacing GPS/atomic clocks, assumptions on loop-free, tolerant of rare loss, multicast/unicast, asymmetry degrades accuracy; security trends and defence in depth, IP as enabler. Requests: mixed L2/L3, determinism, bounded latency/jitter, tight feedback, high availability/low recovery, redundancy/low loss, precise timing, centralised path computation, distributed configuration optional.

### 2.3 Building automation systems (§4)
Management network (IP: BACnet/IP, KNX) over field networks (non-IP: LonTalk, Modbus, PROFIBUS, FL-net; RS232/RS485 timing). BMS/HMI/LC architecture; large buildings with monitoring room, small with remote centre over WAN. Field use cases: environmental monitoring (poll every 100 ms, 99.999 %), fire detection (10–50 ms interval, 10 ms delay, 99.9999 %), feedback control (1–5 ms interval, 10 ms delay, 1 ms jitter, 99.9999 %). Security largely absent today. Requests: < 10–100 ms delay for several hundred devices, < 1 ms jitter, 1–10 ms feedback, up to 99.9999 %, data available in disasters, authentication, integrity/origin authentication, confidentiality to remote devices.

### 2.4 Wireless for industrial (6TiSCH) (§5)
IPv6 over TSCH (802.15.4e) as converged deterministic wireless; shared design principles with TSN (scheduled hops); PCE/NME manage timeslots and device resources for constrained devices; ARQ with two branches resembles PRE (track branches, elimination at the gateway); PCE needs a protocol/data model to get/set schedules (CoAP mapped by a gateway; slotFrame object), energy policies, neighbour reporting; deterministic forwarding PHB. Requests to DetNet: configuration/operations for deterministic paths, end-to-end deterministic forwarding protocols (tagging, IP), a PRE protocol.

### 2.5 Cellular radio (§6)
Fronthaul (BBU–RRH), midhaul (eNB–eNB), backhaul. Fronthaul one-way budget 250 µs of 3 ms LTE processing, 100 µs in NGMN/CPRI (≈ 20 km fibre); queuing/scheduling may dominate on shared short links; DetNet can control bandwidth assignment, scheduling, buffering, isolation between slices. Midhaul CoMP needs 1–10 ms rigid inter-eNB delay. Time sync: delay accuracy ±8 ns (±16 ns round trip), TAE 65/130/260 ns depending on carrier aggregation, ±2 ppb frequency — noted as below the DetNet layer (hardware); transparent/boundary clocks in every node; phase and frequency sync. Loss: FLR < 10^-7 data, < 10^-6 control; retransmission/FEC/redundant streams/protection switching all problematic. Streams equal priority, symmetric links. Reservations must be authenticated and signed. Today: dedicated fibre/WDM fronthaul, IP/MPLS-TP midhaul/backhaul with 1588/syncE, TDM-over-IP (RFC 4553/5086/5087, MEF 8). Future: mixed xHaul, L2 and L3 traffic alike, functional splits (eCPRI, RoE, 802.1CM), PDV ±20 ns, no way yet to signal time-sensitive reservations for L3 flows across the whole stack. Requests: unified xHaul data-plane transport (coexisting diverse flows, deterministic environment, multiple splits, slicing/multi-tenancy, in-band and out-of-band OAM, multiple link technologies); data-flow information models aware of time sensitivity and underlying deterministic services.

### 2.6 Industrial M2M (§7)
PLC↔PLC and PLC↔S-PLC critical control/data streams on a plant LAN; non-critical traffic (up to 80 %) served by prioritisation. Topologies daisy chain, ring, hub-and-spoke, comb. Cycle times 1–100 ms; max end-to-end delivery 100 µs–50 ms; 2–32 streams PLC–PLC (100–700 B), up to 256 S-PLC streams; ≤ 20 % of bandwidth on 1 Gbit/s links; loss tolerance: at most one packet per consecutive cycle (two in a row = network down); availability 99.999 %; time sync 1 µs; streams created ~10 times per day/week/month, increasingly dynamic; security by physical separation and firewalls today. Requests: converged IP, bounded latency/jitter, 99.999 % via redundancy, 100 µs–50 ms delivery, bounded consecutive loss, security.

### 2.7 Mining (§8)
Remote monitoring/teleoperation of autonomous trucks and underground machines; wireless (802.11, leaky feeder, LPWAN) last hop; over-provisioned Ethernet backbone with QoS; SCADA in processing. Future: DetNet in the wired backbone connecting APs, more reliable AP transitions, wired+wireless deterministic end to end. Requests: bandwidth efficiency, very low delay for teleoperation, dedicated bandwidth for video, predictable delay, unified wired+wireless DetNet.

### 2.8 Private blockchain (§9)
Permissioned blockchains; P2MP coordination over persistent P2P connections; challenge is low-latency block propagation, not rate (hundreds of bytes to a few MB/s). Today L2/L3 VPNs without determinism. Requests: L2/L3 multicast of blockchain traffic, bounded low latency and negligible loss, coexistence with IT traffic, distributed control entities.

### 2.9 Network slicing (§10)
Slicing = multiple logical networks on one physical infrastructure. DetNet applicability: hard resource isolation across slices (no appreciable effect from other slices' activity; P2P/P2MP paths with bandwidth and latency guarantees unaffected by others) and deterministic services within slices (slice built with DetNet, or a DetNet-aware application inside a non-DetNet slice). 5G bearer example: eMBB, URLLC, mMTC; URLLC-A and URLLC-B with separate reservations. Non-5G: utilities and pro audio via slices. Limitations: DetNet is P2P/P2MP while slicing needs MP2MP; flow count limited by DetNet scalability (aggregation, queue management changes needed). Slicing was then still "a collection of ideas" (IETF 99 BoF); other candidates FlexE and SR. Requests: isolation through queuing management, service quality customisation and guarantees, security.

### 2.10 Common themes (§11)
11.1 Unified standards-based networks: extensions to Ethernet (TSN) but other transports possible; centrally administered (not plug-and-play, hot swap expected); standardised data-flow information models; L2/L3 integration (TSN islands via routers; RTP over TSN-grade service); IPv4 must get at least the significant benefits; guaranteed end-to-end delivery (no congestion drops; security drops allowed; best effort may drop; no guarantee against media errors); replacement for proprietary deterministic networks; mix of OT and IT traffic; unused reserved bandwidth available to best effort moment to moment but never to other time-sensitive traffic; lower-cost multi-vendor. 11.2 Scalable size (machine to country) and number of flows (may grow faster than nodes; configurable maximum). 11.3 Timing: bounded latency (query path latency, request bounds, worst-case max/min; **the network must reply that the service is not available rather than accept and not deliver**), low latency (wide ranges, nothing architecturally preventing arbitrarily low), bounded jitter, symmetrical path delays. 11.4 High reliability/availability (six to twelve nines; redundant paths with seamless switching; parameters to communicate reliability). 11.5 Security (timing is part of correctness; PRE changes the attack surface). 11.6 Deterministic flows isolated from each other and from best effort even under saturation.

### 2.11 Out of scope (Appendix A)
DetNet scope: centrally controlled enterprise/corporate networks, never the open Internet; time sync maintained by other means (PTP) — open whether applications may state timing expectations to the network. Internet applications (media delivery, gaming 50–100 ms RTT with 5–20 ms transport budget, VR 1–10 ms / remote VR 1–5 ms) are in scope only inside a centrally controlled domain; their requests (unified control for time-critical flows, application-aware flow filtering without 5-tuple matching, low-latency L3 without changing the data plane, delay-aware OAM) are recorded. DRM (link layer), link aggregation with known max latency across the aggregate, and deterministic stream-establishment time are out of scope.

## 3. How Arcana could realise each element on FreeBSD

Reading the numbers against FreeBSD's tools (dummynet with a 1 ms default tick, software timestamps, no TSN, no PREOF, no hardware timing), the use cases sort into three bands:

**Band A — realistically servable (tens of ms, 99.9–99.99 %, failover in hundreds of ms acceptable or app-tolerant):**
- Pro A/V *IT integration and WAN interconnect of TSN islands* at the 33 ms A/V-sync scale (not the 10–15 ms sound-reinforcement or 10 µs beam-forming scale): a slice per production with a `prio`/WF2Q+ class on every Arcana hop, IGMP/MLD snooping on `if_bridge` for multicast segregation (one IPv4 group per MAC: console-enforced allocation), PIM via `pimd` if routed multicast is needed, VLANs/subnets for segregation of low-cost sinks, measured path delays (probes) given to a "central controller" (the console) that moves reservations to longer paths. Buffering gateway = an Arcana edge with larger `slots`.
- Utilities WAN class WC/WD (100 ms / > 100 ms, 1–10 ms jitter, 5–50 s recovery), FLISR (80 ms, 40 ms jitter), FCAG (500 ms), SCADA inter-domain (IEC 60870-5-104 over the operator WAN with bounded latency instead of best effort): the SD-WAN service with a dedicated dummynet class, IPsec, BFD failover, probe TCAs; availability 99.999 % by dual WAN (optical + microwave ≈ two tunnels over two underlays) with steering; "two circuits on two networks" = two tunnels in `sdwan-steer` candidates.
- Building automation *management network* and remote-centre WAN (100 ms polling, 99.999 %), with authentication/integrity/confidentiality via IPsec/WireGuard and per-service DNS/proxy/IDS (`sdwan-dns`, `sdwan-proxy`, `sdwan-ids`); fire detection at 10 ms delay/99.9999 % only within a LAN segment Arcana does not shape.
- Mining backbone connecting APs: dedicated bandwidth class for video (`queue` weight), low-delay class for teleoperation on wired Arcana hops, FIB per tenant; wireless last hop out of scope.
- Private blockchain: a service FIB with a low-latency class, iBGP-distributed routes, L3 multicast via pimd for block propagation (optional), coexistence with IT traffic by WF2Q+ shares; "distributed control entities" = multiple consoles later.
- Network slicing use case: see rfc9543.md/rfc9732.md — isolation by FIB + dummynet class per slice on every Arcana link; MP2MP guarantees via the A2A FIB model (addresses the §10.5 limitation partially: no per-pair guarantee).
- Internet applications inside a controlled domain (gaming 5–20 ms transport budget, VR 1–10 ms) on a campus of Arcana hosts: `prio` class + FQ-CoDel for the rest; "flow filtering without 5-tuple" = DSCP trust or Squid SNI classification.

**Band B — partially servable, honestly labelled "engineered, not guaranteed":**
- Industrial M2M at the 10–50 ms cycle end on a LAN/WAN of Arcana hosts: per-stream `queue` with `slots` sized to the cycle, policing to ≤ 20 % share, loss tolerance of one packet per cycle met statistically by priority; 100 µs–1 ms cycles are not reachable (tick).
- Utilities WB (10 ms, 100 µs jitter, 50 ms recovery): 10 ms latency across our own hops is reachable with `prio` and small queues; 100 µs jitter and 50 ms hitless recovery are not (see Band C).
- Cellular backhaul (not fronthaul/midhaul): slices per operator service, DSCP classes, PTP transport as a prioritised class (PTP accuracy itself limited).
- 6TiSCH backbone router role: an Arcana host could be the IPv6 backbone/border router with a deterministic class, but the PCE/CoAP schedule management of the LLN is not Arcana's domain.

**Band C — not servable (documented in §4).**

## 4. Cannot be mapped (and why)

1. **Teleprotection / current differential / inter-trip (4–10 ms with < 250 µs jitter, 99.9999 %, < 50 ms *hitless* recovery, symmetric delay)** — jitter below the dummynet tick (1 ms default; ~100 µs at hz=10000 with CPU cost), no hitless protection (no PREOF), no sub-ms symmetric-delay control. Not offerable.
2. **IEC 61850-90-12 classes WA and WB jitter/asymmetry/time accuracy (10–100 µs jitter, 1–10 µs time accuracy, zero/50 ms recovery)** — same reasons plus no hardware timestamping.
3. **Cellular fronthaul and midhaul CoMP (100–250 µs one-way, ±8 ns, TAE 65–260 ns, PDV ±20 ns, FLR < 10^-7)** — orders of magnitude below software packet processing; hardware domain.
4. **Industrial M2M at 100 µs–1 ms cycles, 1 µs time sync** — tick, scheduler and sync limits.
5. **Sound reinforcement (< 15 ms total incl. endpoints) and phase-coherent audio (10 µs)** — the first is marginal only on a dedicated LAN with no transit; the second impossible.
6. **Building-automation field networks replacing RS485 (1–5 ms feedback, 1 ms jitter)** — Arcana shapes at L3/L2 on hosts; cannot be the field bus.
7. **6TiSCH schedule management (PCE/CoAP slotFrames, ARQ/PRE tracks)** — outside FreeBSD/Arcana; a border router only.
8. **Pro A/V "one IPv4 multicast group per MAC" enforcement in bridges** — `if_bridge` snooping is limited (no IGMP snooping in base `if_bridge`); multicast segregation needs VLANs or ng_bridge filtering; routed multicast needs pimd (port).
9. **Seamless (hitless) redundancy (PRP/HSR, 1+1, FRER)** — no replication/elimination in kernel; failover only.
10. **"Guaranteed end-to-end delivery" across the public/carrier underlay** — measurement only.
11. **Link aggregation with known maximum latency across the aggregate (A.4)** — `lagg(4)` exists, but per-member latency is not tracked by the kernel; out of DetNet scope anyway.
12. **Deterministic stream setup time (A.5)** — provisioning through console → cblink → ipfw/dummynet/route takes tens to hundreds of ms and is not bounded; out of scope per the RFC too.

## 5. Requirements and recommendations checklist (the use cases' asks, consolidated)

1. Provide deterministic flows from an L3 (IP) interface that coexist with best-effort traffic on one converged OT/IT network (§2.3.3, §7, §11.1.8).
2. Interconnect L2 deterministic islands (TSN/AVB) over L3 with preserved guarantees (§2.3.1, §11.1.4).
3. Offer redundant/alternate paths so no single link failure interrupts a stream; hitless where the use case demands (§2.3.2, §3, §11.4).
4. Make unused reserved bandwidth available to best effort, never to other reserved flows (§2.3.4, §11.1.9).
5. Support traffic segregation for low-power sinks (forwarding rules, VLANs/subnets, multicast with unique MAC↔group) (§2.3.5).
6. Let a central controller measure path delays and re-place reservations to optimise latency and free short paths (§2.3.6).
7. Allow a single buffering gateway so sinks need no extra buffer (§2.3.7).
8. Meet stated numeric bounds per use case (tables 1–12, §4.4, §6.1, §7.2) or reject the request (§11.3.1).
9. Support symmetric (asymmetry-bounded) delay where required (§3.1.1.1.3, §11.3.4).
10. Provide performance management (mandatory in utility tables) and SLA monitoring (§3, §3.3.2.1).
11. Support precise timing distribution (IEEE 1588 profiles) as a network service, even if outside DetNet proper (§3.3.2.3, §6.1.3, A.1).
12. Mixed L2 and L3 topologies; centralised computation of deterministic paths with optional distributed configuration (§3.4).
13. Authentication, integrity/origin authentication and confidentiality between management and field devices, local and remote (§4.4); defence in depth for OT (§3.3.3).
14. Deterministic-path configuration/operations and end-to-end deterministic forwarding protocols reusable by other deterministic technologies (6TiSCH) (§5.4).
15. Authenticate and sign reservation requests, especially across domains (§6.1.5).
16. Unified data-plane transport for diverse flows with in-band/out-of-band OAM over multiple link technologies; data-flow information models aware of time sensitivity (§6.4).
17. Bounded consecutive loss (at most one lost packet per cycle) (§7.2.1, §7.4).
18. Dedicated bandwidth for video, predictable delay for monitoring, efficiency over over-provisioning (§8.4).
19. L2/L3 multicast for group communication with bounded latency; distributed control entities for scale (§9.4).
20. Isolation across slices through queuing management; customisable, guaranteed service quality per slice; security (§10.7).
21. Centrally administered but hot-swappable components; standardised information models; IPv4 support; multi-vendor (§11.1).
22. Scalable number of flows, configurable maximum (§11.2).
23. Communicate reliability/availability parameters within the network (§11.4).
24. Timing is part of correctness: secure against delay attacks; consider PRE's attack surface (§11.5).

## 6. Open decisions and trade-offs for Arcana

1. **Which bands to claim**: market Arcana's deterministic class for Band A (tens of ms, five nines, failover) only, and state Band B as "engineered, best effort beyond"; never claim Band C. This decides product scope and avoids the RFC's explicit warning about accepting what cannot be delivered.
2. **Tick and CPU**: raising `net.inet.ip.dummynet.hz` and pinning network threads for the latency class vs CPU use on small edges.
3. **Multicast support**: ship `pimd`/`ip_mroute.ko` orchestration (pro A/V, blockchain) or stay unicast with ingress replication; `if_bridge` lacks IGMP snooping, so L2 multicast segregation needs VLAN design or `ng_bridge`.
4. **PTP as a managed service**: deliver ptpd/linuxptp configuration and a PTP transport class, documenting ~10–100 µs software accuracy, or leave timing entirely to the operator (the RFC says DetNet does not own it).
5. **Dual-underlay availability model**: how availability is computed and promised when failover is BFD-driven (downtime per event ≈ detection + re-steer); publish the formula.
6. **Per-cycle loss semantics (M2M)**: whether the console can express/verify "no two consecutive losses" from probe samples; probes sample at ≥ 100 ms, so verification is statistical.
7. **Symmetric delay**: pin both directions to one tunnel pair always (simpler, less optimal) or only when asked.
8. **Security baseline for OT**: mandatory IPsec/WG on every tunnel for utility/BAS services, IDS (`sdwan-ids`) default-on, which conflicts with lowest latency (crypto adds µs–ms on small CPUs).
9. **Information model**: adopt RFC 9016/9633 traffic-spec vocabulary even for Band A services so later DetNet-capable hardware underlays can be plugged in without a model change.
10. **Rejection UX**: how the console presents "not available" with alternatives (longer latency bound, lower rate, different edges), per §11.3.1.
