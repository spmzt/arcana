# RFC 9315 — Intent-Based Networking: Concepts and Definitions

IRTF NMRG, Informational, October 2022. Source: `scratchpad/rfc/rfc9315.txt` (read in full).

## 1. Purpose

RFC 9315 fixes the vocabulary of intent so that "intent" stops being a synonym for
"anything above device configuration". It defines intent as declarative operational
goals and outcomes without the how; distinguishes it from service models (RFC 8309) and
from policy / Policy-Based Network Management (event-condition-action rules); lists the
principles an Intent-Based System (IBS) must exhibit (single source of truth, one touch
but not one shot, autonomy with supervision, learning, capability exposure, abstract and
outcome-driven); decomposes IBN functionality into intent fulfillment (ingestion,
translation, orchestration) and intent assurance (monitoring, compliance assessment,
compliance actions, abstraction/aggregation/reporting); draws the intent life cycle with
its inner (autonomic) and outer (human) loops across the user, translation and network
operations spaces; and gives a small taxonomy (operational, rule, service, flow intent).
It is explicitly research-oriented and non-normative. For Arcana it answers "what is the
SD-WAN document, what are workflows and policies, and what would an intent layer be".

## 2. Concepts and definitions, completely

### 2.1 Definitions (Section 2)

- **Intent**: a set of operational goals (that a network should meet) and outcomes (that
  a network is supposed to deliver) defined in a declarative manner without specifying
  how to achieve or implement them. First introduced by RFC 7575 as "an abstract,
  high-level policy used to operate the network"; 9315 insists it is more than a policy.
- **Intent-Based Management**: management based on intent. **IBN**: a network that can
  be managed using intent. **IBS**: a system that supports management functions guided
  by intent (a controller, a management application, or functions distributed across
  the network).
- **IBA** (Intent-Based Analytics): analytics defined and derived from users' intent,
  used to validate the intended state.
- **Statement of Intent / intent statement**: one aspect or component of intent; the
  plural "intents" is discouraged.
- **Policy**: a set of rules that governs the choices in behaviour of a system.
  **PBNM**: Policy-Based Network Management. **PDP** / **PEP**: Policy Decision /
  Enforcement Point.
- **Service Model**: a model representing a service provided by a network to a user
  (RFC 8309: customer service model vs service delivery model).
- **SSoT** (Single Source of Truth): the functional block that normalizes users'
  intent and is the single source of data for lower layers; the set of validated intent
  expressions. **SVoT** (Single Version of Truth): views derived from the SSoT by
  querying, polling, filtering.
- **User**: the operator/administrator, not the end user.

### 2.2 Intent vs non-intent (Section 3.1)

Intent provides *data abstraction* (no nerd knobs) and *functional abstraction* (not
even how to achieve it; the IBS figures out the course of action). Examples of intent:
steer traffic from one geography away from another unless the destination is there;
avoid a vendor's equipment even at reduced service levels; maximize utilization unless
service levels degrade 20% from their historical mean; VPN services have path protection
at all times; generate in situ OAM/telemetry whenever latency fluctuates significantly;
space-network routing minimizing dependency on balloons; smart-city signal traffic on
dedicated redundant slices. **Not** intent: configure an interface with an IP address
(device configuration); emit an alert when utilization exceeds a threshold (a rule);
configure a VPN with a tunnel from A to B over path P (service configuration); deny
traffic to P1 unless from P2 (access policy / firewall rule).

Rendering: ideally by the network itself (distributed) with intent disseminated to all
devices; in practice certain functions are conceptually centralized (a single point of
interaction, global knowledge, intent-agnostic or constrained devices). ONF TR-523's
narrower "declarative controller interface" definition is noted as one implementation,
but a controller API that merely abstracts the network does not qualify as intent;
ingestion may involve dialogs, clarification and refinement rather than request/response.

### 2.3 Related concepts (Section 3.2)

- **Service models**: instantiation involves defining/requesting the service,
  allocating resources (addresses, AS numbers, VLAN/VXLAN pools, interfaces, bandwidth),
  mapping services to resources, maintaining bindings between upper and lower objects,
  and validating/assuring operational state. Orchestration is push-based: controller
  pushes configuration, validates acceptance and derived state. Updating and
  decommissioning must be supported; the device stays service-agnostic. Service
  instances map to lower-layer network/device model instances; services are provisioned
  top-down and troubleshooted bottom-up; business context (billing, orders, SLAs) may
  attach. Unlike intent, a service model does not define an outcome maintained
  automatically; its orchestration logic must be written by the provider/integrator.
- **Policy / PBNM**: a rule = event + conditions + actions; imperative ("what to do when");
  management as a set of simple control loops; suitable to implement self-* behaviour;
  abstraction via a canonical model rendered by a policy agent on controller or device
  (definition decoupled from instantiation and enforcement); push-based deployment, or
  pull via a PDP consulted by a PEP. Unlike intent, rules and courses of action are still
  articulated by users; conflict resolution needs the user or logic outside PBNM; IBSs
  may generate policies that PBNM deploys.
- **Summary (3.2.3)**: service model = data model of service instances with
  dependencies on lower models, orchestration logic outside the model; policy = ECA
  rules rendered by devices without specifying the desired outcome; intent = high-level
  declarative goal at network/service level, outcomes without how, no enumeration of
  events/conditions/actions, algorithms "learned/derived from intent" by the IBS,
  ideally rendered and disseminated by the network itself. Analogy: expert systems
  (policy) vs learning systems (intent).

### 2.4 Principles (Section 4)

1. **SSoT and SVoT**: validated intent expressions are the SSoT; with records of
   operational state it enables comparing intended vs actual, detecting *drift*,
   corrective action, prediction, traceability of whether business goals were met.
   SVoT = views over it.
2. **One touch but not one shot**: the user expresses intent once and the system takes
   over, but reaching a well-formed valid intent is iterative: parse, understand, refine,
   ask for clarifications, moderate between conflicting intent.
3. **Autonomy and supervision**: the system decides within its span of control; the
   user gets reporting and escalation.
4. **Learning**: transfer of reasoning from human to system; learning, reasoning,
   knowledge representation; planning/forecasting.
5. **Capability exposure**: the network's capabilities, requirements and constraints
   must be expressible to compose/decompose intent and map expectations to capabilities.
6. **Abstract and outcome-driven**: users think in outcomes, independent of vendor
   renderings.

Additional aspects: intent targets aggregations (device roles, service types,
topologies), not individual devices; abstraction and inherent virtualization;
human-tailored interaction (the network speaks the user's language); explainability,
conflict detection and IBN-aided resolution, reconciliation of wants vs can-do;
inherent support, verification and assurance of trust.

### 2.5 Functionality (Section 5)

- **Intent Fulfillment**: (5.1.1) *ingestion and interaction* with users: recognize
  intent, refine it, workflows guiding the user, possibly dialog; non-intent APIs may
  coexist for machines. (5.1.2) *translation*: intent to courses of action and requests
  meaningful to provisioning; optimization and learning where several renderings are
  possible (e.g. path computation). (5.1.3) *orchestration*: the configuration and
  provisioning steps across the network.
- **Intent Assurance**: (5.2.1) *monitoring*: events, performance outliers,
  measurements of delivered service levels, telemetry generation and collection.
  (5.2.2) *compliance assessment*: compare observed behaviour with expected behaviour
  held by the SSoT; assess effectiveness and magnitude of fulfillment actions; detect
  *intent drift* (caused by control-plane or lower-level management operations) and its
  severity. (5.2.3) *compliance actions*: corrective actions, or reporting that lets
  operators modify intent and moderate conflicts. (5.2.4) *abstraction, aggregation,
  reporting*: up-level detailed statistics to concepts the user can act on.

### 2.6 Life cycle (Section 6, Figure 1)

```
         User Space   :       Translation / IBS       :  Network Ops
                      :            Space              :     Space
        +----------+  :  +----------+   +-----------+ : +-----------+
Fulfill |recognize/|---> |translate/|-->|  learn/   |-->| configure/|
        |generate  |     |          |   |  plan/    |   | provision |
        |intent    |<--- |  refine  |   |  render   | : |           |
        +----^-----+  :  +----------+   +-----^-----+ : +-----------+
             |        :                       |       :        |
.............|................................|................|.....
             |        :                  +----+---+   :        v
             |        :                  |validate|   :  +----------+
             |        :                  +----^---+ <----| monitor/ |
Assure   +---+---+    :  +---------+    +-----+---+   :  | observe/ |
         |report | <---- |abstract |<---| analyze | <----|          |
         +-------+    :  +---------+    |aggregate|   :  +----------+
                      :                 +---------+   :
```

Two loops: the **inner** loop (IBS space <-> network operations space) is fully
autonomic closed-loop automation counteracting drift and learning how to render; the
**outer** loop extends to the user who adjusts intent on feedback. Intent comes into
being, is refined and modified, and may be retracted.

### 2.7 Taxonomy (Section 7) and security (Section 9)

- Warning against "intent-washing" (calling an SDN controller's NBI an intent interface).
- **Operational Intent**: the real thing. **Rule Intent**: synonym for policy rules.
  **Service Intent**: synonym for customer service model (RFC 8309). **Flow Intent**:
  synonym for a Service Level Objective for a given flow.
- Security facets: securing the IBS itself (verification and validation of intent,
  authenticated and authorized users only, tamper detection, multiple levels of intent
  with security intent at a "deeper" level not modifiable by regular operations,
  explanation of security ramifications); mitigating erroneous or compromised intent
  (detect abnormal behaviour, roll back to a safe state, contain error amplification,
  dynamic supervision and reporting); expressing security policies as intent (data
  governance, confidentiality levels, availability/protection/isolation levels,
  encryption levels, authorized entities).

## 3. How Arcana maps onto each element, and what it would need

### 3.1 Classifying Arcana's artifacts with 9315's taxonomy

| Arcana artifact | 9315 class | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| `sdwan.json`, the MEF 70.2 document (UCS, edges, SWVCs, zones, policies, flows, UNIs) kept by `sdwan.c` | **Service model / service intent** | It describes service instances and their attributes "in a portable, implementation-agnostic way" and the console orchestrates it (plan/apply pushes jobs, validates). Per 3.1 "configure a VPN with a tunnel from A to B" is service configuration, not intent. |
| Policy performance criteria (delay, variation, loss thresholds per application flow) and the manager ordering tunnels by them | **Flow intent** (an SLO per flow) rendered into **rule intent** (`sdwan-steer` candidate lists; TCA thresholds) | The user states the SLO; the console derives the steering tables; the agent enforces (PEP) and fails over locally. This is the closest thing to an inner loop Arcana has. |
| Workflows with triggers (`host-down`, `threshold`, `kmsg`, every N seconds) and conditions | **Rule intent / PBNM** | Event-condition-action chains written by the user; the console is PDP and the agent PEP. |
| History threshold rules (`net:igb0=125000000`) and TCA with damping | **Rule intent** | Pure ECA. |
| SASE security policies (ip-block lists, dns filter, rate limit, url filter) named by AF-SECURITY criteria | **Policy** (MEF 88 security policies) | Access policy / firewall rules. |
| ipfw rulesets, rc.conf edits, jail.conf edits, sysctls, BIRD config | **Device configuration** | Nerd knobs; the lowest layer. |
| Steering state `sdwan-state.json`, `sdwan-steer.json`, `sdwan-rules.json` on the agent | rendered state (network ops space) | What the translation produced. |

Arcana today has **no operational intent layer**. It is a service-model orchestrator
with policy-based automation, which is a solid base (9315 §3.2.2: "it is conceivable for
IBSs to generate policies that are subsequently deployed by a PBNM system").

### 3.2 Principles and functions, present or absent

| 9315 element | Arcana today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| SSoT | the versioned service document (a change carries the version it was made from; stale writes refused) is a true single source for SD-WAN; workflows.json, lists, identities likewise | not one SSoT across jails/services/network outside SD-WAN; the host's own files (rc.conf, jail.conf) are edited in place and are also "truth" |
| SVoT / views | Paths, Faults, Security tabs; `/api/sdwan/apply` history; audit trail | views are per feature, no cross-cutting "intent compliance" view |
| Drift detection | `sdwan_edge.c` recomputes what each edge should have and takes away/remakes what differs from `sdwan-state.json`; `jail -e` read-back after libjailconf edits | compares intended vs *what the console made*, not vs *what the host actually runs* (an operator editing ipfw or rc.conf by hand is invisible until the next apply). No continuous assessment. |
| One touch, not one shot | the validator (`[Rn]` checks) refuses and explains; plan before apply | no refinement dialog, no conflict moderation between services/policies beyond uniqueness checks |
| Autonomy and supervision | local failover on probe/BFD without the console; jobs survive disconnects; faults, TCA, push/mail notifications, audit trail | autonomy limited to steering; supervision good |
| Learning | none | — |
| Capability exposure | `hello` (host facts), `plugins` (which programs exist: BIRD, strongSwan, Squid, dnsmasq, wg), `transport`, `kernel` | not modeled as capabilities an intent translator can query (e.g. "can this edge do IPsec GCM in hardware", "has racct") |
| Abstract/outcome-driven | the user writes MEF attributes, not ipfw | still "how" (which tunnel kinds, which FIB numbers, which prefixes) |
| Explainability | the trace tree per change (api/auth/daemon/lib/exec steps with argv and output) is excellent explainability of *fulfillment* | no explanation of *why* a tunnel was chosen or a TCA raised beyond the event data |
| Intent targets aggregations | edges are grouped by service; policies by zone | yes, partially |
| Rollback / safe state | ipfw `apply` safe mode (old rules wait in set 1 and swap back unless confirmed), `job-confirm` | not generalized to all changes (routes, BIRD, SAs) |
| Multi-level security intent | API tokens read-only; settings and enrol tokens excluded from tokens; HMAC-chained audit | no "deeper level" for security policy that ordinary operations cannot override |
| Monitoring | probes (MEF 105 metrics), BFD, events, history, record, faults | see rfc9232.md |
| Reporting/aggregation | faults with severities, TCA, counts per security policy | no roll-up to "service X compliant / degraded" |

### 3.3 Proposal: an intent layer for Arcana

1. **Intent statements as a YANG-modeled document** above the MEF service document.
   Keep the service document as the *rendered* service model (SSoT of services); add an
   `intent.json` (SSoT of intent) with statements such as: "sites {A,B,C} form one
   private network; voice between them: one-way delay < 30 ms, loss < 0.5%, encrypted
   always, prefer private underlay, fail over within 1 s"; "every edge reachable by the
   console out of band" (which an ACP, rfc8994.md, fulfils); "Internet breakout local
   where the site has it, else via the nearest hub"; "no plaintext management on any
   edge". Translation (console) renders these into MEF attributes: SWVCs, zones,
   application flows with performance criteria, UCS preferences, tunnel kinds,
   policies, BFD timers. Keep the MEF document editable too (service intent stays a
   first-class input for users who want it), but mark which parts are derived.
2. **Assurance loop with drift detection**, enabled by the YANG redesign: with NMDA
   datastores (RFC 8342) on the agent, the console compares `intended` (what it
   rendered) with `operational` (what the host runs: actual ipfw rules via `ipfw list`,
   routes via netlink, SAs via setkey/swanctl, BIRD protocols). Any difference is
   *intent drift* and becomes a fault (`drift`) with the diff as description; the
   compliance action is re-apply (with the existing safe mode) or report. Today's
   `sdwan-state.json` comparison becomes the `applied` datastore.
3. **Compliance assessment per intent statement**: evaluate SLOs against probe/BFD
   samples per flow and tunnel (TCA already does the arithmetic), aggregate to
   "statement compliant / at risk / violated since T", and show it as the top-level
   view (SVoT). Feed violations back into translation (choose another underlay, change
   BFD timers, raise a new tunnel) — the inner loop.
4. **Refinement dialog in the PWA**: when a statement is ambiguous (two sites, three
   underlays, no preference) or conflicts with another (same prefix in two services,
   SLO impossible on the only available underlay as measured), the translator asks,
   lists ramifications, and records the choice in the statement.
5. **Capability exposure as data**: extend `hello`/`plugins` into a capabilities tree
   (crypto offload, racct, divert, bpf taps, available programs, FIB count, address
   families, link speeds) that the translator consults before rendering.
6. **Rollback generalized**: every job that can cut connectivity gets the ipfw-style
   two-phase commit (make, wait for confirmation reachable through the new state, else
   revert), so compliance actions can act autonomously without lock-out.
7. **Security intent at a deeper level**: security policies and the ACP/management
   reachability statements require a stronger credential (a second WebAuthn ceremony or
   a role on the token) and are never overridden by ordinary service edits; the audit
   trail already provides tamper evidence.
8. **Vocabulary**: use 9315's words in the UI and docs: *Intent* (the statements),
   *Services* (MEF document), *Policies/Rules* (workflows, thresholds, SASE policies),
   *Configuration* (host files). Do not call the REST API an "intent interface".

## 4. Cannot be mapped / not applicable

- **Learning systems / AI translation**: out of scope for a C project; the
  translator will be algorithmic (rules plus optimisation), which 9315 accepts as a
  first stage.
- **Natural-language ingestion and human-machine dialog**: only a structured form
  with refinement questions is realistic.
- **Vendor-independent abstraction**: Arcana is single-vendor (FreeBSD); abstraction
  from implementation is still valuable (gre vs wg vs uswe chosen by the translator) but
  "independent of vendor-specific renderings" does not arise until Mplify 119 interop.
- **Decentralized intent rendering by the network itself**: requires an autonomic
  control plane and ASAs (rfc8994.md, rfc9222.md); initially the console is the IBS.
- **Business context (billing, orders, tariffs)**: a single-user fleet manager has none.

## 5. Checklist Arcana could be measured against

1. There is a validated intent store that is the single source of truth, versioned,
   separate from rendered service and device configuration.
2. Every rendered artifact (service attribute, job, rule) can be traced back to the
   intent statement(s) that caused it.
3. Intent statements declare outcomes (SLOs, reachability, protection, placement
   preferences) without naming tunnel kinds, FIBs, rule numbers or addresses.
4. Translation produces a plan the user can inspect before orchestration (exists for
   SD-WAN: `GET /api/sdwan/plan`).
5. Ambiguous or conflicting statements are detected and resolved with the user, and the
   resolution is recorded.
6. Operational state is continuously compared with intended state; drift raises a fault
   with the difference.
7. SLO compliance is assessed per statement from measurements, with a defined evaluation
   window and damping, and reported at the statement level.
8. Corrective actions run without a human for a defined class of violations, with
   rollback to a known-safe state when they fail.
9. The system exposes its capabilities and constraints in a machine-readable form used
   by translation.
10. Users receive reporting that relates outcomes to their intent (compliant, at risk,
    violated, since when, why).
11. Security-related intent lives at a protected level requiring stronger authorization.
12. All intent operations are authenticated, authorized and audited (exists: WebAuthn,
    tokens, HMAC-chained audit trail).
13. Retraction of an intent statement removes everything it caused and nothing else.
14. The documentation does not label non-intent interfaces as intent.

## 6. Open decisions and trade-offs

- **Where the SSoT lives**: console files (today) vs a datastore shared by several
  consoles. Tied to the multiple-controller decision in rfc7426.md.
- **How declarative to go**: too abstract and the translator must guess (which
  underlay, which encryption); too concrete and it is just the MEF document again.
  Suggested: statements fix outcomes and constraints, the translator fixes mechanisms,
  the plan shows both.
- **Drift handling**: auto-remediate (risk: fighting an operator's manual fix) vs
  report-only by default with per-statement opt-in to auto-remediation.
- **Datastore model on the agent**: NMDA intended/applied/operational is clean but
  requires the agent to read back the live state of every subsystem it configures
  (ipfw, routes, SAs, BIRD, WireGuard, jails); most readers exist already.
- **Explainability vs autonomy**: the trace model explains what was done; intent
  rendering needs an additional "why" record (which measurements, which alternatives
  were rejected).
- **Granularity of SLO evaluation**: per flow per tunnel (what probes give) vs per
  application end to end (what the user cares about; needs end-to-end measurement,
  e.g. STAMP between UNIs, see rfc9232.md).
